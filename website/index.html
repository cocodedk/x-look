X Look
فارسی

A reading mode for X that you can open at work

X Look is a small Chrome extension that takes the furniture off x.com. The left nav, the trends, Grok and the ads are gone. What is left is one quiet column of posts, and the pictures stay grey until you ask for them.

Download x-look.zip Source on GitHub

What it changes on x.com

  • No left nav

    It is removed, not tucked behind a hover. On a narrow window X's own bottom bar stays, so you can still get around.

  • No right rail

    Search, “Subscribe to Premium”, today's trends and who-to-follow all go.

  • No Grok, no messages drawer

    Both stay out of the corner of your eye.

  • No promoted posts

    Ads are dropped by their exact label, so an ordinary post that happens to say “promoted” stays.

  • A calmer timeline

    One 660px column in the middle, posts set in a serif at 17px with room between the lines, hairline separators, and the like and repost buttons dimmed until you hover.

  • Filter posts

    A small box in the empty space left of the column. Type a few words and only posts containing all of them stay.

  • Pictures and videos wait for you

    Each one becomes a small grey tile. Click a tile and only that item opens; a video starts playing when you do. A post with four photos opens one photo at a time.

  • Your theme stays yours

    X Look only changes layout and type. Light, dim and dark all keep working.

Install it in Chrome

  1. Download x-look.zip and unzip it.
  2. Open chrome://extensions.
  3. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  4. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder.
  5. Reload x.com.

Works in Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers. Click the X Look icon in the toolbar to switch it off and on; it remembers your choice.

Good to know

X changes its page often. X Look holds on to the few hooks X keeps stable, such as the banner, the primary column and the tweet itself, rather than generated class names, so it should rarely break. If it does, please open an issue on GitHub.

It asks for no permissions beyond running on x.com and twitter.com and remembering whether it is on, and nothing you read leaves the page.
